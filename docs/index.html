About Hero Dirt Forecast
Experimental trail-moisture forecast for Connecticut
×

Hero Dirt Forecast uses a three-layer soil-water model to estimate trail-surface moisture across Connecticut. The model represents the trail tread (0–2 cm), shallow soil (2–5 cm), and deeper soil (5–20 cm). Water can infiltrate into the tread, move downward through the soil profile, drain from the deeper layer, and exchange between the shallow soil and trail surface through time.

Landscape information

v2 Beta

This version uses a Penman-style atmospheric drying formulation for the trail-tread layer during both the analysis and forecast periods. Drying responds to temperature, humidity, wind, solar geometry, cloud cover, terrain exposure, and vegetation.

The tread drying coefficient is currently provisional and is being evaluated against field observations. Freeze-thaw processes are not yet represented explicitly.

The model accounts for spatial differences in soil properties, terrain, and vegetation. Soil information includes sand and clay content, field capacity, wilting point, saturation water content, saturated hydraulic conductivity (Ksat), and Plasticity Index (PI). Terrain information includes elevation, slope, and terrain exposure, while vegetation includes tree canopy, shrub, and herbaceous cover.

Observed and forecast weather

The regional soil-moisture state is constrained using NASA SMAP (Soil Moisture Active Passive) satellite observations. SMAP provides a broad regional estimate of upper-soil moisture and is not a direct measurement of the trail tread.

Recent rainfall is ingested from NOAA MRMS (Multi-Radar/Multi-Sensor System) precipitation analyses.

Future conditions are driven by National Weather Service (NWS) forecasts including air temperature, relative humidity, wind, cloud cover, and precipitation.

How the Hero Dirt score works

The Hero Dirt score is based primarily on modeled water content in the upper 2 cm of soil. Tread moisture is evaluated relative to the hydraulic properties of the local soil.

F = (θtread - θWP) / (θFC - θWP)
where:

θtread = volumetric water content of the modeled 0–2 cm trail-tread layer
θWP = local soil water content at wilting point
θFC = local soil water content at field capacity
F = dimensionless normalized hydraulic state

F = 0 corresponds approximately to wilting point and F = 1 to field capacity.

H = 100 × Sdry × Swet × Sdeep
where:

H = Hero Dirt score, approximately 0–100
Sdry = penalty for excessively dry trail tread
Swet = penalty for excessively wet or deformable tread
Sdeep = penalty when deeper soil remains wet even after the immediate surface begins drying

Why soil texture and plasticity matter

The wet-condition penalty depends in part on soil texture and soil plasticity. Texture describes the relative abundance of sand, silt, and clay, which influence infiltration, drainage, and water retention.

Plasticity is represented using the Plasticity Index (PI), an engineering measure of the range of water contents over which fine-grained soil behaves plastically. More plastic soils receive a stronger wet-condition penalty because they are more likely to become soft or deformable as moisture rises.

Trail-condition classes

The modeled state is translated into six classes: Very Dry / Dusty, Dry / Firm, Moist / Good, Hero, Wet, and Too Wet. Dry / Firm, Moist / Good, and Hero are generally favorable riding conditions. Current score relationships and thresholds are empirical and will be evaluated against rider observations.

Primary model inputs

SMAP — NASA Soil Moisture Active Passive satellite soil moisture
MRMS — NOAA Multi-Radar/Multi-Sensor observed precipitation
NWS — National Weather Service forecast temperature, relative humidity, and 6-hour precipitation
Landscape properties — soil hydraulic properties, texture and plasticity, terrain, slope, and vegetation
Experimental forecast

Hero Dirt Forecast operates on an approximately 200-m landscape grid. Landscape data therefore describe the environment surrounding the trail rather than the exact engineered trail tread.

Trail construction, drainage, rock exposure, soil compaction, canopy shading, microtopography, and localized precipitation may cause actual conditions to differ from the model.

This is a beta forecast. Field validation is ongoing, and individual trails may differ substantially from the surrounding 200-m model grid cell.
